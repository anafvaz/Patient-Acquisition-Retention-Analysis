# Patient-Acquisition-Retention-Analysis
This project evaluates the return on investment of five patient acquisition channels for a mental health clinic offering therapy services. The goal is to identify which channels deliver the highest patient lifetime value relative to acquisition cost, and to inform where the clinic should concentrate its marketing spend.

## Data
The analysis uses synthetic data modelled on realistic clinic operations, covering patients, appointments, payments, and monthly acquisition costs across four years (2022–2025). No real patient data was used.

## Key Findings
GP Referral and Referral - Patient are the strongest channels by a significant margin, with LTV:CAC ratios of 12.7 and 9.1 respectively — meaning every €1 spent returns €12.7 and €9.1 in patient revenue. Organic Search is marginally viable at 3.1. Paid Social and Corporate Partnership both fall below 1, meaning the clinic spends more acquiring these patients than it recovers in lifetime revenue.
Patient retention drops steeply regardless of channel — more than 50% of patients do not return after their third session.

## Recommendation
GP Referral and Referral - Patient should receive the majority of acquisition investment — both deliver strong returns and require relatively low spend. Strengthening relationships with GPs and incentivising existing patients to refer others are low-cost levers worth prioritising.
Paid Social and Corporate Partnership both return less than €1 for every €1 spent. Without further data it is not possible to determine the root cause. A dedicated analysis of these two channels is recommended to identify whether the issue is correctable, if not, ceasing investment should be considered.
